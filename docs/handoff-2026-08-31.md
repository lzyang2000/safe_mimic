# Safe Mimic LiDAR Avoidance Handoff

Updated: 2026-08-31

## Executive summary

The current prototype has two distinct unresolved problems:

1. **The joint-level safe-reference filter is not reliably obeyed by the
   executed policy.** The privileged filter can request an appropriate arm
   posture, such as lowering an extended arm, but the learned action head was
   free to ignore or counteract that request. An explicit arm residual was just
   added to the final joint command, but the current checkpoint predates this
   path and has not adapted to it.
2. **Avoidance has a time-to-collision (TTC)/control-authority limit.** A visible
   approaching human normally affects the policy at the first completed 10 Hz
   scan, so the dominant problem is not a two-second sensing delay. The robot
   often needs much longer to generate useful outward body motion. Collision
   probability remains above 50% until scheduled TTC is roughly 2.5 seconds,
   and fast or close approaches fail most often.

Blind spots are a known limitation of the physical LiDAR arrangement and are
not the immediate target. The next iteration should focus on visible, fast
approaches and on proving that the policy executes the filtered limb command.

## What was built

### Scene and motion

- The nominal MjLab G1 tracking task was extended with a randomized crowd and
  one articulated primary human that can approach/intersect the robot.
- Training uses prebuilt packed human trajectory banks instead of compiling
  human motion online for every environment step. This removed the principal
  rollout bottleneck found during profiling.
- Crowd members use simplified capsule bodies; the moving primary human retains
  the full capsule fit. Viser can show the human mesh for visual inspection,
  while collision, LiDAR, rewards, and termination operate on capsules.
- The robot reference is live-aligned to the robot before applying the safe
  reference filter. This is necessary because the no-state-estimation tracker
  does not accurately retain global root position over long motions.
- Crowd radius, density, shape, human size, and obstacle-free scenes are
  randomized. The primary human is spawned from the surrounding population and
  moves toward the robot. Robot-link-to-human capsule clearance, rather than a
  root-distance proxy, drives proximity and collision checks.

### LiDAR and deployable actor contract

- The sensor is a Mid-360-like, head-mounted ray sensor.
- Scans update at 10 Hz and are held for five 50 Hz policy steps.
- Returns inside 0.3 m and beyond 5.0 m are excluded.
- The current dense source pattern has 185 azimuth by 27 elevation samples.
- The actor adapter pools this to 120 azimuth by 9 elevation cells.
- Each cell contains normalized range and range rate: `120 * 9 * 2 = 2,160`
  directional values, followed by one normalized scan-age value.
- The actor receives only deployable tracking/proprioceptive observations and
  held LiDAR. It does **not** receive privileged human capsules, CBF geometry,
  or teacher corrections at inference time.
- The critic, rewards, terminations, and auxiliary loss may use privileged
  simulator state.

The explicit directional range-rate observation was added because a single
held range snapshot does not expose approach velocity clearly. Positive range
rate means the closest return in a directional cell is closing.

### Reference filters and training signal

There are two privileged safe-reference corrections:

- A planar root-velocity correction that moves the reference away from people.
- A 29-joint position residual produced by link-level capsule avoidance and a
  short-horizon arm-posture search.

The arm posture search is batched across environments. It evaluates candidate
shoulder/elbow/wrist velocities with finite forward-kinematics lookahead, so a
move orthogonal to the instantaneous CBF gradient, such as dropping a
horizontal arm, can be selected. Its current link-filter settings include a
0.8 m activation distance and a 0.5 s posture lookahead.

The auxiliary actor head predicts 31 values from deployable observations:

```text
2D body-frame planar correction + 29 joint-position residuals
```

The correction is supervised from privileged filter output during training,
but is predicted from tracking state and LiDAR at deployment. Privileged
corrections are not concatenated into the actor observation.

The environment also retains the original MjLab tracking rewards at their
original weights. Additional terms reward safe planar velocity, safe progress,
filtered joint tracking, and survival, and penalize human/link proximity and
freezing. Robot-link-to-human clearance below 0.1 m terminates the episode.

## Problem 1: filtered joints are not obeyed

### Observed failure

In play, the privileged joint filter can request an arm correction, but the
robot does not lower or redirect the limb strongly enough. This is especially
clear when an arm is held level and a human approaches it: the desired behavior
is an aggressive shoulder-led drop, while the executed policy tends to keep
tracking the nominal pose or make a weak/incorrect correction.

### Root cause

Originally, the auxiliary head's predicted joint residual was concatenated to
the policy MLP features. The final MLP remained free to ignore it or produce an
opposing normalized joint action. Auxiliary target accuracy therefore did not
guarantee command compliance.

### Most recent implementation change

An explicit residual path now adds the predicted correction to the final policy
command:

```text
normalized_action += 0.5 * arm_mask * predicted_residual_rad / G1_action_scale
```

It currently affects the 14 shoulder, elbow, and wrist joints. Legs and waist
remain policy-controlled to avoid destabilizing locomotion before the arm path
is validated. The fixed joint mask and action scales are non-persistent buffers,
so older checkpoints still strict-load. The export/ONNX actor uses the same
path.

**Important:** `model_21000.pt` was trained before this explicit residual path
existed. It can be loaded to inspect the mechanical effect, but it is not valid
evidence that the new path has been learned. Fine-tuning or retraining is
required.

### Required diagnostics before changing the filter again

Log the following per side, on active avoidance frames:

1. Teacher joint residual versus predicted joint residual, including MAE and
   cosine agreement.
2. Predicted residual versus the residual actually added to the normalized
   action.
3. Added action residual versus achieved joint motion over the following
   0.1-0.5 seconds.
4. Fraction of active frames where shoulder/elbow/wrist motion improves actual
   link clearance.

This separates three possible failures: bad privileged target, bad auxiliary
prediction, or good prediction that is not realized by the low-level policy.
Only after that should the residual gain be increased beyond 0.5 or enforcement
be extended to waist/legs.

## Problem 2: TTC and fast/close approaches

### Corrected reaction-envelope benchmark

The benchmark used 2,048 parallel environments with only the primary human,
no crowd, no pushes/noise, initial radius 0.75-4.0 m, scheduled intercept
0.5-4.0 s, and six-second episodes. Collision is based on minimum
robot-link-to-human surface clearance below 0.1 m.

Overall, 1,149 of 2,048 cases collided: **56.1%**.

| Scheduled intercept TTC | Collision rate |
|---|---:|
| < 1.5 s | 86.7% |
| 1.5-2.0 s | 60.6% |
| 2.0-2.5 s | 55.7% |
| 2.5-3.0 s | 42.7% |
| 3.0-3.5 s | 37.4% |
| >= 3.5 s | 26.7% |

| Nominal average approach speed | Collision rate |
|---|---:|
| < 1.0 m/s | 40.7% |
| 1.0-1.5 m/s | 56.2% |
| 1.5-2.0 m/s | 63.1% |
| >= 2.0 m/s | 91.6% |

### Detection versus useful motion

The benchmark compares the normal actor action with the same observation after
blinding LiDAR. Their actions diverge at 0.08 s for p10, p50, and p90: the first
completed 10 Hz scan is normally affecting the action.

The first time the robot reaches at least 0.25 m/s outward velocity is much
slower and more variable:

| Percentile | First useful outward motion |
|---|---:|
| p10 | 0.14 s |
| p50 | 0.46 s |
| p90 | 1.56 s |

Collision rate by time remaining between first useful outward motion and the
scheduled intercept is:

| Remaining margin | Collision rate |
|---|---:|
| 0-0.5 s | 98.4% |
| 0.5-1.0 s | 84.2% |
| 1.0-1.5 s | 54.7% |
| 1.5-2.0 s | 37.9% |
| 2.0-2.5 s | 28.4% |
| >= 2.5 s | 21.0% |

The visible human is therefore usually sensed promptly. The limiting factors
are the learned maneuver, acceleration/control authority, and limb response.
Scheduled TTC must be roughly 2.5 seconds before collision drops below 50%, and
roughly 3.5 seconds before it drops below 30%. The remaining 21-27% collision
floor with ample warning is consistent with poor maneuver selection and the
joint-compliance problem, not just insufficient warning time.

### Recommended next training distribution

- Sample the primary-human encounter explicitly by TTC instead of sampling
  distance and speed independently.
- Oversample 1-3 s TTC, close starts around 0.75-2.0 m, and approaches from
  1.0 through at least 2.5 m/s.
- Start a curriculum around 2.5-4.0 s TTC and shorten it as success improves,
  while retaining a fixed fraction of hard, fast cases throughout.
- Adaptively upweight collision and low-clearance cases rather than letting
  easy dance-away episodes dominate the rollout.
- Add an early lateral/outward-displacement objective, evaluated shortly after
  the first visible closing return, rather than rewarding only eventual safe
  velocity.
- Track joint-filter compliance alongside collision bins so improved root
  avoidance does not hide unresolved limb contacts.

## Benchmark correctness note

Checkpoint loading restores `common_step_counter`. Older benchmark scripts
scheduled the primary human before loading the checkpoint, which made the
recorded initial TTC invalid after the clock jumped. The three policy benchmark
scripts now reset once after checkpoint loading and reschedule the human against
the restored clock. Do not use older artifacts to infer initial TTC unless they
were regenerated after this fix.
